The Daylily Pause

Something I’m trying to do to slow down

Yesterday, I noticed an orange daylily blooming beside our garage.

It was tucked near the hydrangeas, right there by the place where I park. I have probably walked past that same spot more times than I can count without paying much attention to it.

But yesterday I stopped.

I took a picture.

At the time, I just thought it was a pretty flower. Then I found out that each daylily bloom usually only lasts about a day.

That made the whole thing hit a little differently.

That flower showed up, did its job, made me stop long enough to notice it, and then started to fade. It didn’t need to last forever to matter. It just needed to be seen while it was there.

I’ve been trying to slow down lately. Trying to be more present. Trying to not carry work, stress, noise, and unfinished thoughts straight through the door into my family.

So now I think those daylilies are going to become a small checkpoint for me.

Not some dramatic reflection hour beside the garage.

Just a pause.

Park the car.
See the flowers.
Take a breath.
Walk inside on purpose.

Some days there may be a fresh bloom. Some days there may not be. Either way, it is a reminder that small things are still happening right in front of me, whether I notice them or not.

And maybe that is the point.

Maybe presence is not built in huge life-changing moments.

Maybe it starts beside the garage, with one flower that only had a day, doing exactly what it came to do.
