Denison Pequotsepos: The Little Adventure That Was Severely Underestimated

By Cody Spencer

Our family went to a free winter wildlife class in Mystic and found live-animal exhibits, hands-on learning, and more trails than we had time to explore.

Last week I hastily planned to take the kids on an adventure because Cassie was going to be out of town and the only way to manage these tiny hurricanes is to let them run until they pass out.

So I did some quick Googling and came across a free program called Animals Preparing for Winter being hosted by the Denison Pequotsepos Nature Center. Perfect... A little nature center visit, a class for the kids, and maybe a short hike.

Side note: please never ask me to say the name of this place out loud because, as a Southern transplant, I will absolutely butcher it and most other New England names.

Anyway, we decided to wait until Cassie got back because, of course, the kids wanted to see her and she was dying to see them. No problem, the class was at 2 so we would go to the class and then do a quick “hike” afterward.

Cassie got home from the airport, made herself a sandwich, and was back on the road before she even had time to unpack. Everyone threw their water in my backpack, we piled into the van and were headed to Mystic to check out a nature center.

Bigger on the Inside

As we walked inside, we were greeted by the lady at the front desk. Well, Cassie and I were greeted, the kids were already halfway down the entryway, yelling at each other to come look at whatever they’d found.

We hadn’t even had a chance to find out where the class was being held and they were off.

Now, from the outside, the building really didn't look like much. I was picturing a few exhibits, maybe some animals, and enough stuff to keep the kids entertained until the class started. It turns out there was a whole lot more going on inside than I expected.

There were displays about the woods and wildlife around here, live animals, and plenty for the kids to investigate on the way downstairs. Every time one of them found something, the other two needed to see it immediately.

The kids found plenty to look at before we'd even made it to the class.

The Class That Started All of This

The whole reason we came was the free Animals Preparing for Winter program, and I have to say, it was great.

Ashley, our instructor, had the kids learning about all kinds of ways animals handle winter without making it feel like we had dragged them to school on a weekend. We talked about snowshoe hares changing colors, wood frogs surviving the cold, bears getting ready for winter, and how owls manage to fly so quietly.

There was even an opossum in the classroom who came out while we were there. Of course, the kids were all about that.

One of the residents who made a pretty big impression.

At one point Ashley used a piece of string to help explain how the frays on an owl’s feathers help them stay silent in flight. We also learned that owls can’t move their eyes around the way we do. Now when I say “we” learned that, I mean I turned to Cassie and said, “I didn’t know that.” She looked me dead in my soul with such a look of disbelief and said, “Haven’t you stared an owl in the face before?” What?! I’m sorry, I didn’t realize I was in a minority of people who have apparently never won a staring contest against an owl. Apparently that’s a normal thing we should all have done.

Back to the point… There were things the kids could actually handle and investigate, too, which was a lot more effective than expecting three kids to sit still and absorb an entire lecture.

But my favorite part was watching them get excited about meeting the animals. They got to touch several turtles, a black snake, and even a Madagascar hissing cockroach.

Yes. A cockroach.

Now, Kylie wasn’t exactly thrilled about holding that one at first, but she eventually worked up the courage to do it, even if she had to close her eyes in the process. Now if you knew Kylie, you would understand that her holding a cockroach is not some small feat. Meanwhile, Corbin was ready to volunteer for just about anything.

They were having an absolute blast. And honestly, so was I.

Apparently There Are Trails Everywhere

To be honest, I saw “Nature Center,” saw that it advertised trails, and that it was in Mystic. Planning done… How big could a nature center with “trails” in Mystic really be?

Well, let me tell you. I should've researched a little more.

The Nature Center and nearby Coogan Farm connect through more than ten miles of trails. Ten. Miles. And I had shown up thinking we might take a quick walk.

The posted map made me realize just how much there was to explore. If you're planning a visit, here's the Nature Center's official trail map so you can see all those routes for yourself.

The class was over and the kids were headed in three different directions and I had to “just pick” a trail. Except there is no such thing as just picking a trail when I’ve got a wife in boat shoes, Kaysen halfway down some yet-to-be-identified trail, Kylie climbing a rock, and Corbin… wait, where did Corbin go?

Normally, I overplan every outing. But I hadn’t even counted this as an “outing,” so I did almost no actual planning for this trip other than booking the class, getting the address and adding it to my calendar.

We ended up deciding on the Forest Loop (Red Trail), which the Nature Center says begins behind its main building. Once we spotted the red square trail blaze, we were in business.

We spent about an hour wandering around the pond, letting the kids climb around on the rocks and investigate whatever caught their attention. There was a boardwalk through the wetlands, and at one point all three of them were lying down by the water trying to see what was in it.

Sometimes the rocks and the pond are the whole adventure.

We also got to see the owls and hawks outside. I could've spent more time looking around there, too.

We didn’t even finish the full trail. It was starting to get cold, and Cassie’s Sperrys were definitely not hiking shoes. Remember, she had just gotten off a plane, eaten a sandwich, and gotten right back in the van.

Kaysen was not happy when we turned around. He wanted to keep going, and I pretty much had to drag all three of those kids out of the woods and back into the van.

Which is funny, because this whole thing started with me just trying to find somewhere to run the energy out of them. An hour in the woods later, they were still ready for more.

So, Was It Worth It?

Absolutely!! I went into this expecting a small nature center, a little class, and maybe a quick walk. What we found was a place the kids didn't want to leave, with way more to explore than we could fit into one afternoon.

They learned some things, got to touch animals they'd probably never get that close to otherwise, climbed on rocks, explored a pond, and complained when it was time to go home. That last part might be the best review a family outing can get.

A big thanks to Ashley and the team at Denison Pequotsepos Nature Center for a great afternoon. We’re already looking forward to coming back.

Planning a Visit

A few useful details from DPNC's visitor information as of November 16, 2026:

Location: Denison Pequotsepos Nature Center, 109 Pequotsepos Road, Mystic, CT 06355.

Museum hours: Sunday–Friday, 10 a.m.–4 p.m.; Saturday, 9 a.m.–4 p.m.

Regular museum admission: Adults $10; children ages 3–12 $7; seniors ages 65+ $7. The Nature Center also charges admission for the outdoor birds-of-prey exhibits; those are not part of the free trail access. Individual programs may have different fees or registration requirements. Our Animals Preparing for Winter class on November 15 was free with advance registration, based on our own registration and visit; this did not establish free general museum admission.

Trails: Free, dawn to dusk. The Nature Center and Coogan Farm connect through more than ten miles of trails; we explored only part of the Forest Loop (Red Trail). Our walk took about an hour, including rocky ground and a wetland boardwalk, so I would wear shoes that are comfortable for walking.

Before you go: Check dpnc.org for current hours and programs, registration, and the official downloadable trail map. Dogs and other pets are not permitted on trails around the main Nature Center; leashed dogs are allowed on the marked trails at Coogan Farm.

We visited November 15, 2026. Visitor hours, admission, and trail information were checked against the Nature Center’s official website on November 18, 2026; programs and policies may change.
