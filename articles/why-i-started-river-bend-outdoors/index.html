I Don't Know Where River Bend Outdoors Is Going. But I Know Why I Started.

By Cody Spencer

Why River Bend Outdoors gives me a reason to get outside, follow my curiosity, keep learning, and make time for what matters.

Somewhere between researching hiking trails, chasing down random questions, and finding excuses to go fishing, I realized River Bend Outdoors might be something I need more than I expected.

It usually starts with a map.

Maybe I'm trying to find somewhere new to take the kids hiking. Nothing complicated. Just a nice place to spend a few hours outside, explore a little, and hopefully get everybody home without too much complaining.

Simple enough, right?

Except somewhere between looking at the trails and figuring out where to park, I notice something interesting.

Maybe it's an old road cutting through the woods. Maybe it's a piece of property history I didn't know about. Or maybe there's something on the map that doesn't quite make sense.

Now I've got questions.

And if there's one thing I've learned about myself, it's that I have a hard time leaving a question unanswered.

Before long, I've got several maps open, I'm researching the history of the property, comparing access points, and trying to figure out whether something I found online is actually accurate.

At some point, what started as a simple family hike has turned into something resembling the reconnaissance phase of an assault on a hostile mountaintop.

Which is probably a little excessive considering I'm just trying to take three kids for a walk in the woods.

But here's the thing.

I love it.

I genuinely enjoy digging into things, figuring out how they work, and learning something I didn't know before. One question leads to another, and suddenly I'm researching something that has almost nothing to do with why I opened the map in the first place.

And usually, somewhere along the way, I find something worth sharing.

I suppose that's part of how River Bend Outdoors came about.

Although, if I'm being fair, there's a little more to it than that.

Old Habits Are Hard to Break

I spent a good portion of my life in environments where planning, preparation, and paying attention to details were pretty important.

Between my military service in Explosive Ordnance Disposal (EOD) and my time as a first responder, I got used to researching things, thinking several steps ahead, and trying to understand what I was getting myself into before I got there.

Apparently, that doesn't just go away.

Even when nobody is asking me to do any of it, I still find myself studying maps, evaluating routes, and trying to understand the places around me.

The difference is that now I get to choose what I'm investigating.

And I get to follow my curiosity simply because I enjoy it.

Some of my favorite work in EOD involved building complex training scenarios. I'd study maps, figure out how people were likely to move through an area, anticipate what the opposing side might do, and think through how we'd respond. I also had to understand the terrain and the rules of the training area—what we were allowed to do, what we weren't, and how those limits shaped the plan.

I loved that part. There was always another detail to chase down, another possibility to consider, and another piece of the puzzle that changed how everything fit together.

I loved teaching, too, especially getting outside to teach land navigation and helping people develop skills they could actually use.

There's something satisfying about watching somebody figure something out for themselves.

Designing those scenarios and teaching other people kept my own skills sharp. When you're responsible for making an exercise realistic or explaining a skill to somebody else, you tend to make sure you understand it pretty well yourself.

Since leaving the military, I haven't practiced some of those skills nearly as often as I used to.

Some have gotten rusty.

And somewhere along the way, I also stopped making as much time for the things I enjoyed doing.

I don't think it happened all at once.

Life just gets busy.

There's work, the house, the kids, appointments, schedules, and a seemingly endless list of things that need to get done. You take care of one responsibility, and there's usually another one waiting behind it.

And before you know it, the things you enjoy doing have become things you'll get around to eventually.

Except eventually has a funny way of never showing up.

The Perfectly Legitimate Excuse

Here's something I've realized about myself.

I'm pretty good at finding reasons to do things that need to be done.

I'm not nearly as good at making time for something simply because I want to do it.

Which is where River Bend Outdoors comes in.

Let's say I'm working on an article about a hiking trail.

I've done the research. I've looked at the maps. I've read whatever information I can find.

But I'm missing a few things.

Maybe I need photographs of the trail. Maybe I want to verify where an access point actually is. Maybe there's something I noticed on a map that I'd rather see with my own eyes than take somebody else's word for.

Well, now I've got a problem.

I can't very well finish the article without going out there, can I?

So off I go. I mean, that's the only responsible answer... right?

Maybe it's twenty minutes walking around a park. Maybe it's a few hours exploring somewhere new. Maybe I bring the kids along and turn it into a full day outside.

The important part is that I'm out there.

I'm doing something I enjoy.

And somehow, calling it research for River Bend Outdoors makes it feel a little easier to justify.

It's probably ridiculous that I sometimes need to call something a business to give myself permission to go outside and have fun.

But apparently, that's where we're at.

And you know what?

I'll take it.

Because whether I come home with enough information to finish an article or nothing more than a few pictures and another question to investigate, I usually feel better than I did before I left.

Sometimes I need to clear my head.

Sometimes I need some time to think.

And sometimes I just want to be outside without worrying about the next thing I'm supposed to be doing.

I don't always need to accomplish something.

I'm still working on remembering that.

Three Kids, a Trail, and the Best-Laid Plans

Of course, one of the things I want most out of River Bend Outdoors is the opportunity to share those experiences with my family.

I want my kids to grow up knowing what it's like to explore somewhere new.

I want them hiking trails, catching fish, wandering around parks, getting curious about something they found, and learning that there's a lot more to the world than whatever happens to be on a screen.

I want them to know how to read a map, figure things out, and feel comfortable being outdoors.

But more than any particular skill, I want us to have those experiences together.

Now, I also have three kids.

So I'm under no illusion that every outdoor adventure is going to look like something out of a travel magazine.

Somebody's going to be hungry.

Somebody's going to complain about walking.

Somebody will probably need a bathroom five minutes after we passed the last one.

And there's a pretty good chance that whatever carefully researched plan I came up with will start falling apart before we've even gotten very far.

That's family life.

I can spend hours learning everything there is to know about a trail, but none of that guarantees everybody's going to be in a good mood when we get there.

And that's okay.

Or at least I'm trying to get better at remembering that it's okay.

Because the point isn't to execute some perfectly planned outdoor adventure.

It's to spend time together.

It's to give the kids opportunities to explore, discover things, make mistakes, and have stories to tell afterward.

And maybe, somewhere along the way, I'll learn to enjoy the unexpected parts a little more myself.

I know I'm a better version of myself when I've had some time outside doing something I enjoy.

I'm more patient with the kids, more present with my wife, and less caught up in everything that needs to get done. I'm better able to appreciate our time together and be the husband and dad I want to be.

That's something I want more of.

Not just for myself, but for my family.

Following the Next Question

So where does all of this leave River Bend Outdoors?

That's the part I'm still figuring out.

For a while, I've been trying to decide exactly what RBO is supposed to be.

Is it an outdoor recreation website? A place to share family adventures? A collection of hiking guides and fishing reports? Somewhere to write about things I've researched and places I've explored?

Probably some combination of all of those.

And knowing how my brain works, probably a few things I haven't even thought of yet.

One week I might be researching a hiking trail. The next I might be comparing ski mountains for a family trip or trying to understand the history of a local park.

Maybe I'll write about a fishing trip that went completely differently than I expected.

Maybe I'll revisit something I learned in the military and see how much I still remember.

Or maybe I'll spend an unreasonable amount of time answering a question that most normal people would have been perfectly content to leave unanswered.

That's just how I operate.

What I do want is for the things I share to be useful.

If I've actually visited a place, I want to tell you what I found there.

If I'm researching somewhere I haven't been yet, I want to be clear about that, too.

And if I discover something interesting along the way, I'd like to share it in case somebody else finds it interesting.

Maybe it'll help another family plan a trip.

Maybe it'll give somebody an idea for a place to go fishing.

Maybe it'll answer a question they've been wondering about.

Or maybe somebody will read something I've written and decide it's time to get outside themselves.

That would be pretty cool.

Remembering Why I Started

I'd love for River Bend Outdoors to grow into something successful.

It would be incredible if, someday, it could help support my family and give me more opportunities to spend time doing the things I love.

I'm not going to pretend that possibility doesn't interest me.

But I've also started to recognize something important.

I don't want to spend so much time trying to make River Bend Outdoors successful that I end up losing the very thing that made me want to start it.

I don't need another responsibility that eats up every spare minute of my life.

I don't need another project that keeps me sitting inside, staring at a computer, wondering why I never have time to go fishing.

I've already got plenty of things competing for my attention.

What I need is something that gives me a reason to step away from all of that once in a while.

Something that keeps me curious.

Something that encourages me to keep learning, practicing old skills, and sharing what I've learned.

Something that gets me out of the house, onto the water, into the woods, and spending more time with my family.

For a while, I wasn't making enough room in my life for those things.

I'd like to change that.

And maybe River Bend Outdoors is how I start.

I still don't know exactly what it's going to become.

Maybe it'll grow into a business. Maybe it'll become a collection of stories, photographs, maps, and places I've explored. Maybe it'll turn into something I can't even picture right now.

I'm interested to find out.

But for the first time in a while, I'm starting to think I don't need to know where all of this is going before I can enjoy getting there.

Besides, I've got an article I'm supposed to be finishing.

And wouldn't you know it?

I'm missing a few pictures.
