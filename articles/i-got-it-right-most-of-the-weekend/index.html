Embracing Ordinary Moments: A Weekend of Connection and Reflection

A weekend of kid-speed hiking and backyard play, with a reminder to appreciate the ordinary moments.

Finding Calm in the Chaos

If I’m being honest, weekends are usually where I struggle. Not because weekends are bad, but because they often feel like walking around with a mental to-do list permanently running in the background.

  • The lawn needs cutting.
  • Something in the garage needs fixing.
  • The house feels behind.

Something always needs to be cleaned, moved, organized, repaired, or “handled.” Even when I’m physically with the family, part of my brain is usually somewhere else thinking:

“I should probably be…”

This weekend felt different.

A Game Night to Remember

Friday night, we had a game night with the kids before bed. Nothing elaborate—just hanging out together, laughing. I did start getting frustrated while trying to explain to Corbin why you can’t drive a monster truck toy over Monopoly houses. But then I took a breath because I’m pretty sure, in his mind, he was at Monster Jam. No big lesson—just a good night.

Saturday Adventures

Saturday started out with Kaysen’s lacrosse. While Kaysen was out on the field sweating like he was trying out for a professional contract, Corbin had apparently created his own sporting event nearby. He spent part of the morning running the baseball bases next to the lacrosse field for absolutely no reason other than:

“Why not?”

Then at one point, he picked up his little lacrosse stick and announced it was his “pogo stick.” What followed was apparently an Olympic-level pole vault competition where he used it to “vault” himself over first base. In his defense, the commitment was impressive. Honestly? That kind of stuff is exactly what I’ve been missing.

Not because I don’t care, but mostly because life moves fast, and my brain is usually somewhere between house projects and whatever thing I think needs to get done next. Instead of rushing through the day, Cassie and I prepped the night before:

  • Gear packed.
  • Cooler loaded with sandwiches and snacks.
  • Baseball stuff ready.

We built enough margin into the day that we didn’t have to sprint from one thing to the next.

Slowing Down to Connect

I’m realizing something about myself: when things feel chaotic, I get intense. I rush. I start feeling like we’re behind even when nobody else thinks we are. Without realizing it, I stop noticing what’s actually happening around me.

So after lacrosse, instead of rushing somewhere or defaulting to sitting at home feeling like we should be doing something, we slowed down. Playground first, then a short hike later. At one point, we were full-on pretend pirates, voices and all. I hit Kaysen with an absolutely terrible dad joke:

“What’s a pirate’s favorite jewelry? Arrrrring.”

He actually laughed, which, if you’re a dad, is basically equivalent to getting a standing ovation.

The Joy of Exploration

Then we hit the trail. And by “trail,” I mean 1.3 miles of complete kid-speed nonsense. Corbin had to step on every rock. Not some rocks—EVERY rock. Like there was a consequence if one got skipped. At one point, Corbin just stopped and screamed:

“E-I-E-I-O!”

I laughed and asked him what he was doing. He claimed he saw a snake and was scaring it away. Apparently, Old MacDonald has now been added to official wilderness survival procedures.

To me, it was a short local hike. To Corbin? We were exploring an undiscovered wilderness. Meanwhile, Kaysen turned into my assistant trail guide. I showed him how to look at the map and figure out where we needed to go. At one point, I let him decide if we should finish the whole loop around the pond.

He looked at the map, thought about it, looked at Corbin moving at approximately glacial speed, laughed, and said:

“Probably not because Corbin is s..l..o..w.”

Part explorer, part tiny operations officer, still very much an 8-year-old kid. And honestly? I noticed things about my boys that I think I’ve been missing. How much Kaysen genuinely likes the outdoors. How naturally helpful he is without being asked. How funny Corbin is. How much personality shows up when I slow down enough to actually notice it.

A Relaxed Evening

By the time we got to baseball, Corbin had completely emptied the tank.

Mud on his pants, hat still on, completely asleep in the backseat after his thrilling expedition. So while Kaysen warmed up with the team, I sat in the car and let Corbin sleep. And for once? Everything was fine. No stress. No pressure. No: “Come on buddy, we gotta go.” Because we had time.

A Simple Sunday

Sunday was simpler. Cassie took the kids to the beach while I cut grass for a few hours. Later, we all worked together trying to reset the house for the week while the kids turned the trampoline into what can only be described as: a backyard foam party sponsored by poor decisions.

  • Sprinkler.
  • Dish soap.
  • Chaos.
  • No bath needed.

Parenting win.

The Power of Ordinary Moments

The thing I keep thinking about from this weekend is how normal all of it was. Nothing big. Nothing extraordinary. Just ordinary family life. But I was calmer, more present, and less mentally somewhere else. Because of that, I noticed more—the personalities, the jokes, the random conversations, the weird little things that are easy to miss when my brain is busy thinking about the next project.

I’m still figuring all of this out, but I’m really proud of how I showed up this weekend. I think the kids had a pretty good time too. It’s these ordinary moments that often hold the most meaning, and I’m grateful for the chance to embrace them.

In the end, it’s about being intentional. It’s about showing up fully and appreciating the little things that make life rich. This weekend was a reminder that sometimes, the best moments come from simply being present.
