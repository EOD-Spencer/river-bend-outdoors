Darrow Pond: A Park Worth Watching

By Cody Spencer

A quiet East Lyme pond, a protected piece of open space and a new trail grant that could make the place easier to explore.

There's an open field and a Goodwin Trail information board near the Mostowy Road entrance. At first glance, it might look like a small place to stop on the edge of East Lyme. Beyond that entrance, though, lies a much bigger piece of protected open space—with a pond, woods and a history worth knowing.

It's already a place with a story to tell. Now there's another reason to pay attention: Connecticut has awarded $405,000 for trail improvements that could make more of this place easier to enjoy.

What you can find today: East Lyme lists the Richard H. Goodwin Trail approach at 16 Mostowy Road, and a section of the trail was rerouted in 2024. The new accessible trail and parking improvements are planned and funded, not confirmed built or open. Check current notices and signs before setting out.

The property was once farmland. A 2013 environmental review says J.C. Penney later used the site to test small motorized equipment. Developers had other plans for it after that. What actually happened was very different.

The pond from the shoreline, with lily pads along the edge.

How the land was saved

The land was once slated for an 18-hole golf course. Later, a 600-unit active-adult community with a mix of detached homes, multifamily housing, and condominiums was approved. East Lyme voters supported buying the land instead. In December 2011, The Trust for Public Land announced the purchase was complete.

The purchase protected 301 acres, including the pond and surrounding woods. It wasn't just about having a place to walk. The land is near the town's aquifer protection area, so keeping it undeveloped also helps protect drinking water.

By 2023, East Lyme was looking at recreation improvements on about 100 acres under Parks and Recreation planning. The other roughly 200 acres are subject to a conservation easement, according to the town's 2022 meeting record. That helps explain why the plans focus on one part of the property, not all 301 acres.

The property outlined in red on the Town of East Lyme's 2012 map.

The open field near the entrance to Darrow Pond.

The Goodwin Trail connection

The information board at the parking lot shows the Richard H. Goodwin Trail. East Lyme lists the Darrow Pond Trail as an approach to that longer route. So this isn't just a place to see the pond; it's also connected to a wider trail network.

The Goodwin Trail maps posted at the information board.

What the grant could make possible

There's already been a change to the trails here. According to the Eightmile River Watershed, about a quarter-mile section near the pond was rerouted in September 2024 because of erosion. That earlier reroute is separate from the larger trail and parking project the town is planning now.

By December 2025, East Lyme had a bigger project on paper. The town's engineering plans lay out work along the trail network, including an accessible trail and improvements near the entrance. Here's the area those plans cover:

East Lyme's December 2025 trail-improvement plan. View a larger version or the complete town drawings.

The Zoning Commission gave the project conditional approval on March 19, 2026. That came with requests to rethink parts of the construction approach and reduce the proposed parking area, so the December drawing isn't necessarily the final layout.

Then, on October 1, 2026, the state awarded East Lyme $405,000 for Darrow Pond Trail Improvements. The grant covers construction, maintenance and education. For visitors who haven't explored the area—or who would appreciate a more approachable walking route—the town's proposed accessible trail and entrance improvements offer something to look forward to.

The plans and grant show where the town wants to take the place. They aren't a completed trail or a promised opening date. But after years of protecting the land, making it easier for more people to experience could be an important next chapter.

A place to know now—and revisit later

This place doesn't have to wait for new construction to be interesting. It's part of a 301-acre conservation story, with woodland, water and a connection to the Goodwin Trail. The entrance may be modest, but what the town protected here is not.

The new grant adds a hopeful possibility: better ways to reach and enjoy that landscape without losing sight of why it was saved. The details will matter as the town moves from plans toward actual work, and any finished improvements deserve to be reported when they're real.

For now, the pond is worth getting to know for what it already is—and worth remembering as East Lyme works toward what it might become.

Before you go

East Lyme lists the trail approach at 16 Mostowy Road. The town trail directory and Goodwin Trail updates are good places to check before visiting, along with signs on site.

I met some hikers from the Meshomasic Hiking Club who said they were setting out on a seven-mile hike. They had high-visibility vests on for hunting season. That's a good reminder to pack a fluorescent-orange vest or hat before a fall hike. Connecticut DEEP recommends bright clothing to help others see you in the woods.
