Well, It's Been a Minute

By Cody

Life got busy. Here's why River Bend Outdoors matters to me, and why I'm making room for the experiences that started it.

If you've followed River Bend Outdoors for a while, you've probably noticed things have been a little quiet around here.

Okay, maybe more than a little.

No, I didn't disappear into the wilderness, and I certainly haven't been spending all this time fishing.

I wish I had a more exciting explanation, but the truth is pretty simple.

Life happened.

Most mornings start with getting kids ready for school and onto the bus. Somewhere in there, I'm usually high-fiving my wife as she's coming home from a night shift while I'm headed out to drop Corbin off and make my way to work.

And for the record, I'm absolutely not a morning person. I love fishing, but I'm not exactly jumping out of bed at 4 a.m. to watch the sunrise from a kayak.

Throughout the workday, though, my mind tends to wander.

I think about places I'd like to explore, getting back out on the water, and what it would be like to turn River Bend Outdoors into a successful business.

I think about slowing down a little, enjoying the outdoors more, and being more present with my family instead of constantly thinking about what needs to get done next.

Then I get home.

There's Kylie's dance class, Kaysen's lacrosse practice, homework to get through, dinner to figure out, and whatever else three kids have managed to add to the schedule.

Before I know it, the day's over.

And when I finally get a chance to sit down, there's usually a mix of relief that I can finally relax and that nagging feeling that another day has slipped by.

Another day spent thinking about all the things I'd like to do instead of actually doing them.

Tomorrow, we'll do it all again.

Sound familiar?

That's part of why River Bend Outdoors matters so much to me.

I've always enjoyed fishing, exploring new places, and spending time outside. When I started RBO, I wanted somewhere to share those experiences and connect with people who enjoyed the same things.

It seemed pretty simple.

Get outside, have some adventures, share what I learned, and hopefully build something worthwhile along the way.

Then came the website ideas, the projects, the plans, and all the things I thought River Bend Outdoors could become.

Somewhere along the way, building RBO started taking up more time than actually doing the things that made me want to start it.

I was spending an awful lot of time thinking about the outdoors for somebody who wanted to spend more time enjoying them.

Kind of defeats the purpose, doesn't it?

But I've also realized something.

I need River Bend Outdoors.

Not just because I want to build a business, although I absolutely do.

I need it because without something giving me a reason to make plans and follow through, it's easy to fall into the routine of simply getting through each day.

There's always another responsibility. Another project around the house. Something that needs fixing, cleaning, or figuring out.

Even when I'm home with the kids, there's usually something in the back of my mind telling me I should be working on the yard or getting something else done.

That's one of the things I love about getting the family away from the house.

When we're out exploring somewhere, the lawn isn't staring at me. The dishes aren't sitting in the sink waiting to be washed. I can focus on the kids and whatever they've decided is interesting that day.

Of course, getting to that point isn't always easy for me.

I've spent a lot of my life operating with a plan and anticipating what might go wrong. When things start feeling rushed or uncertain, it's pretty easy for me to slip right back into military mode.

We should've left twenty minutes ago. Is that weather moving in? Does this trail connect? Are we going to have enough time to get back?

Half the time, we're only running late according to some schedule I've made up in my own head.

That's where planning helps.

If I've done a little research beforehand, know the area, and have a general idea of what to expect, I don't have to spend the whole outing worrying about what's next.

I can build in some extra time, figure out the important details, and then let the adventure unfold.

And sometimes, the best parts are the things you never planned.

Take a fishing trip with the boys to Miller's Pond.

We had a couple of hours while the girls were busy, so I figured we'd head out, explore a little, and do some fishing.

I brought three fishing rods.

I also ended up carrying those three fishing rods through the woods considerably more than we actually used them.

At some point, the boys discovered a rocky embankment near the water and decided throwing rocks into the pond was a lot more entertaining than fishing.

So I found myself a rock at the top, sat down, and watched.

We probably spent more time throwing rocks than fishing, but I didn't care.

I had nowhere else to be. We weren't trying to cover a certain distance or make it to some destination. They were having fun, and I was perfectly happy just watching them.

Of course, eventually I decided to demonstrate what happens when you throw a REALLY big rock into the water.

Nearly threw my back out.

But the splash and the looks on their faces?

Totally worth it.

We didn't catch a bunch of fish. We didn't accomplish anything particularly impressive.

But I came home feeling better because I'd spent that time with my boys.

And that's exactly the kind of experience I want more of.

Not every adventure needs to be some incredible expedition. Sometimes getting out for a couple of hours, exploring somewhere new, and letting the kids decide what happens next is more than enough.

For me, River Bend Outdoors gives those experiences a purpose and a place in my life. It gives me a reason to research a new fishing spot, make plans to explore somewhere, and actually follow through instead of just thinking about it.

And if I can share something useful with somebody else along the way, even better.

That's what I want to get back to.

More time fishing, exploring, and enjoying the outdoors. More opportunities to discover places around southeastern Connecticut and share what I learn along the way. And hopefully a few good stories about the adventures that don't go remotely according to plan.

I have a feeling I'm not the only one who struggles to make time for those things.

Maybe you've got a fishing rod sitting in the garage that hasn't seen the water in months. Maybe there's a trail you've been meaning to check out, or somewhere you've wanted to take the kids but haven't gotten around to it.

I get it.

I'm right there with you.

I'm not relaunching River Bend Outdoors because I've suddenly figured out how to balance everything. The calendar hasn't magically emptied, the responsibilities aren't going anywhere, and tomorrow morning is still going to come way too early.

I'm doing it because I don't want to keep putting the things I enjoy on the back burner while waiting for life to slow down.

I want to build something that helps me make time for those experiences, share what I learn, and hopefully encourage a few other people to get out there, too.

And maybe, as we go, we'll help keep each other motivated.

So, if you've been here before, thanks for finding your way back. If you're new, welcome aboard.

I've still got plenty of ideas for where River Bend Outdoors can go. I'm just trying to make sure I enjoy some of the journey this time.

See you out there!

Cody
